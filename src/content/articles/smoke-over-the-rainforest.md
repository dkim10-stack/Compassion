Every year, the same thing happens in Sumatra and Kalimantan. The dry season arrives, the land burns, and the sky turns orange and gray. This year it is no different.

## What’s happening right now

Fires burned more than 200,000 hectares in Indonesia between January and July, and smoke from Sumatra and Kalimantan keeps crossing borders. By early September, the air in Palangka Raya, Central Kalimantan, was off the charts. Its pollution index hit 3,540, and anything above 300 counts as hazardous. Thick haze and poor visibility blocked aerial firefighting. Crews found 263 fire spots in a single day across six priority provinces

The warnings came early. In June, a Singapore think tank issued a rare “code red” alert for severe haze in August and September.

The fires also hurt the climate. NASA reported that after only about a month, the 2026 fires had already released roughly a tenth of the greenhouse gases from the devastating 2015 season. Much of that comes from peatland. Indonesia holds about 36% of the world’s tropical peatland. When peat burns, it releases carbon that took thousands of years to build up. It also smolders underground for weeks, so crews struggle to put it out.

## People start these fires

Indonesia’s disaster agency chief says human activity is the main cause. Police have opened 219 criminal investigations and named 162 suspects. Land clearing is the top motive, and investigators found that some fires were set on purpose. Fire is the cheapest way to clear land for plantations and farms. That links the problem to palm oil, paper and pulp, which means it links to you.

Yet,some things have improved. Reforms have kept the burning below the levels of 2015 and 2019. But prevention gaps remain a decade after the worst haze crisis, and accountability is thin. Between 2015 and 2017, officials revoked only three licences over fire violations. One expert says real progress needs steady enforcement and affordable alternatives to burning for local communities.

## What this does to everyday people, and to children

For most of us, a forest fire is a news photo. For people living in the smoke, it’s a daily health emergency.

Haze carries fine particles called PM2.5. They’re small enough to enter your bloodstream. They worsen asthma and bronchitis, strain your heart, and hit the elderly, pregnant women and outdoor workers hardest. Studies of the 2015 haze crisis estimated tens of thousands of early deaths across the region.

Children take the worst of it. They breathe faster than adults, so they take in more dirty air for their size. Their lungs are still growing, and repeated exposure can leave damage that lasts a lifetime. Picture a child who spends weeks every year coughing, with burning eyes, repeated infections and nowhere safe to play outside. That’s normal life for kids in haze zones.

School suffers too. When the air turns hazardous, classes change or stop. Sarawak in Malaysia has already faced hazardous air and possible changes to school operations. Parents miss work when kids get sick, and medical bills pile up. A Jakarta think tank estimates that fires from January to August could cost Indonesia between 39.29 and 123.1 trillion rupiah in economic and health losses. Behind those numbers are real families.

The haze doesn’t stop at borders either. Smoke has hurt air quality in Malaysia, Singapore and the Philippines. Indonesia says it’s working with its neighbors through the ASEAN haze agreement. Diplomacy moves slowly but the fires don’t wait.

## Why you need to care now

Burned peatland doesn’t grow back on any timeline that matters. The carbon it releases adds to global warming, and that makes the next dry season harder. One ecologist says fire prevention needs steady effort all year, not just when risks peak. Waiting for the next crisis doesn’t work.

You might think this is Indonesia’s problem. It isn’t. The land being cleared often supplies products you’ll find in your supermarket, your bathroom and your office. What you buy, what you invest in and what you ignore all connect you to these fires. That connection gives you some power.
